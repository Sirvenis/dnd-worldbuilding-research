## 1. D&D Ownership and Corporate History

### Timeline
- **1974**: Gary Gygax and Dave Arneson publish Original D&D (OD&D) through TSR, Inc. (Tactical Studies Rules)
- **1977**: AD&D 1st Edition released (Monster Manual, Player's Handbook 1978, DMG 1979)
- **1989**: AD&D 2nd Edition (David "Zeb" Cook); Dragonlance and Forgotten Realms settings flourish
- **1997**: TSR faces insolvency, purchased by Wizards of the Coast (WotC)
- **1999**: Hasbro acquires WotC for ~$325 million (all-stock deal)
- **2000**: D&D 3rd Edition launches with the d20 System and Open Gaming License (OGL)
- **2003**: D&D v3.5 revision
- **2008**: D&D 4th Edition (tactical, grid-based, divisive)
- **2014**: D&D 5th Edition (current era begins — simplicity, narrative focus, broad appeal)
- **2022**: Hasbro acquires D&D Beyond from Fandom for $146.3M
- **2024**: Revised 5th Edition core rulebooks (Player's Handbook Sept 2024, DMG Nov 2024, Monster Manual Feb 2025)
- **2025**: SRD 5.2 released under Creative Commons CC-BY-4.0

### Key Corporate Facts
- **Wizards of the Coast (WotC)** owns D&D, headquartered in Renton, Washington
- **Hasbro** is the parent company (acquired WotC 1999)
- WotC also owns Magic: The Gathering
- D&D has 85+ million players worldwide as of December 2024
- WotC posted $816M revenue in 2020, growing 24% year-over-year
- The 2023 OGL controversy (see below) was a major community crisis

### The OGL Controversy (2023)
- Jan 2023: Leaked "OGL 1.1" proposed royalties (25% for high earners) and WotC control over derivative works
- Massive community backlash — D&D Beyond subscription cancellations, publisher exodus
- Feb 2023: WotC fully retreated, released SRD 5.1 under Creative Commons (irrevocable)
- 2025: SRD 5.2 released under CC-BY-4.0 with 2024 rules content
- Creative Commons license means WotC can never "take back" the core rules
- Iconic creatures (Beholder, Illithid, Displacer Beast, Yuan-Ti, Githyanki) remain WotC proprietary IP
- Third-party publishers can freely use mechanics, classes, spells under CC license

## 2. D&D Editions History

| Edition | Year | Key Features |
|---------|------|-------------|
| OD&D | 1974 | 3 booklets, 3 classes (fighting-man, magic-user, cleric), 4 races, 3 alignments |
| AD&D 1e | 1977-79 | Core trio format established, expanded classes/races, Gygax-authored |
| AD&D 2e | 1989 | Streamlined, "thief" renamed "rogue", settings era (FR, Dragonlance, Dark Sun) |
| D&D 3e | 2000 | d20 System, OGL, unified basic+advanced lines, WotC's first edition |
| D&D 3.5 | 2003 | Balance refinement, expanded DMG and MM |
| D&D 4e | 2008 | Tactical grid combat, MMO-influenced, divided community, Pathfinder emerged |
| D&D 5e | 2014 | Advantage/disadvantage, bounded accuracy, narrative focus, massive growth |
| D&D 2024 (5.5e) | 2024 | Weapon Mastery, standardized subclass timing, species (not races), 400 spells |

### 2024 Revision Key Changes
- "Races" renamed to "Species" (10 core: Aasimar, Dragonborn, Dwarf, Elf, Gnome, Goliath, Halfling, Human, Orc, Tiefling)
- Half-Elf and Half-Orc dropped from core; Aasimar, Goliath, Orc added
- Subraces eliminated entirely; ability scores now come from Background, not Species
- Weapon Mastery: new combat mechanic (Cleave, Vex, Topple, Push, Slow, Graze, Flex, Sap)
- Subclasses standardized to 4 per class, all unlock at 3rd level
- 16 backgrounds, each granting an Origin feat
- Nearly 400 spells in PHB (up from 361 in 2014)
- Free Rules available on D&D Beyond (all 12 classes with 1 subclass each, 333 spells, 48 monsters)
- Exhaustion simplified: -2 per level to all d20 rolls, death at level 6
- Hide is now flat DC 15 (no more contested Perception)
- Grappling/shoving became plain attacks against fixed DC

## 3. Core Rules Mechanics (5e/2024)

### The d20 System
- Roll d20 + ability modifier + proficiency bonus vs. DC (checks) or AC (attacks)
- Ability scores: STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS, CHA (modifiers range -5 to +5)
- Proficiency bonus: +2 at 1st level to +6 at 20th level
- Advantage/Disadvantage: roll 2d20, take higher/lower (replaces stacking modifiers)

### Three Pillars of Play
1. **Combat** — tactical encounters, initiative, actions, spells
2. **Exploration** — travel, discovery, survival, dungeon delving
3. **Social Interaction** — NPCs, persuasion, deception, intimidation

### Death and Dying
- 0 HP = unconscious, begin Death Saving Throws
- 3 successes = stable; 3 failures = death
- Natural 1 = 2 failures; Natural 20 = regain 1 HP and consciousness
- Damage at 0 HP = auto failure; crit damage = 2 failures
- Instant death if damage at 0 HP equals or exceeds max HP

### Spellcasting
- Spell slots (1st-9th level), regained on long rest
- Cantrips (0-level, at-will)
- Concentration: one concentration spell at a time; damage triggers Con save (DC 10 or half damage)
- Ritual casting: some spells castable without slot, takes 10 min
- 8 schools of magic: Abjuration, Conjuration, Divination, Enchantment, Evocation, Illusion, Necromancy, Transmutation

### RAW vs RAI
- RAW = Rules as Written (literal text)
- RAI = Rules as Intended (design intent, clarified via Sage Advice)
- DMG p.235: "The D&D rules help you and the other players have a good time, but the rules aren't in charge."
- DM is final arbiter — rules are a framework, not a binding contract

## 5. D&D Cosmology — The Great Wheel

### Structure
- **Material Realms**: Material Plane + two echoes (Feywild, Shadowfell)
- **Transitive Planes**: Ethereal Plane, Astral Plane (passage between planes)
- **Inner Planes**: 4 Elemental Planes (Air, Earth, Fire, Water) + Para-elemental Planes
- **Outer Planes**: 17 planes aligned to moral/philosophical axes
- **Positive/Negative Planes**: Raw forces of life and death, enfold the whole cosmology

### Key Planar Features
- **Sigil**: City of Doors, floats above the Spire in the Outlands, ruled by the Lady of Pain
- **Feywild**: Marvelous mirror of Material Plane, time distortion, archfey domains, Fey crossings
- **Shadowfell**: Grim mirror, Domains of Dread (Ravenloft), shadow crossings at death-touched places
- **Astral Plane**: Silvery sea, color pools to other planes, petrified dead gods, soul transit
- **Ethereal Plane**: Fogbound, Border Ethereal overlaps Material, Deep Ethereal has color curtains

### All D&D Worlds Share the Multiverse
- Forgotten Realms (Toril), Dragonlance (Krynn), Greyhawk (Oerth), Eberron, Dark Sun (Athas) — all Material Plane worlds
- "Dream of the Blue Veil" spell (Tasha's) explicitly connects all worlds
- Each world has its own pantheon and cosmology, but all exist in the same multiverse
- DMs can choose which cosmological model applies to their campaign

## 7. DragonLance / Krynn Deep Dive

### Krynn's Five Ages
1. **Age of Starbirth** — Gods forge the world from primordial chaos; dragons created
2. **Age of Dreams** — Knights of Solamnia founded, Mages of High Sorcery established, Third Dragon War (Huma Dragonbane defeats Takhisis with the first dragonlance)
3. **Age of Might** — Istar rises as world power; Kingpriest grows fanatical; mages persecuted; ~1000 years of civilization
4. **Age of Despair** — The Cataclysm (fiery mountain destroys Istar); gods abandon the world; 300 years of famine; War of the Lance (Takhisis returns, Heroes of the Lance defeat her); Legends trilogy (Raistlin's time travel and godhood quest)
5. **Age of Mortals** — Chaos War; Dragon Overlords; War of Souls; Takhisis slain; magic changes

### The Cataclysm
- The Kingpriest of Istar demanded the gods grant him power to eradicate evil
- The gods responded by dropping a fiery mountain on Istar
- This shattered the continent, created the Blood Sea, killed millions
- Gods withdrew from the world for ~300 years
- True clerical magic vanished until the War of the Lance

### Krynn's Pantheon — Three Families
**Gods of Good** (led by Paladine/Bahamut):
- Paladine (Platinum Dragon, father of good, law, justice)
- Mishakal (healing, fertility, mother figure)
- Kiri-Jolith (war, justice, patron of Solamnic Knights)
- Habbakuk (sea, marine life, animal life)
- Branchala (music, forests, elves)
- Majere (meditation, monks, insects)
- Solinari (good magic, white moon, patron of White Robe wizards)

**Gods of Neutrality** (led by Gilean):
- Gilean (keeper of the Tobril — the book of the High God's plan)
- Lunitari (neutral magic, red moon, patron of Red Robe wizards)
- Reorx (forge, crafting, dwarves)
- Chislev (nature, wild places)
- Shinare (commerce, wealth)
- Sirrion (fire, change, passion)
- Zivilyn (wisdom, cosmic awareness)

**Gods of Evil** (led by Takhisis/Tiamat):
- Takhisis (Queen of Darkness, five-headed dragon, control/domination)
- Sargonnas (war, vengeance, minotaurs — consort to Takhisis)
- Morgion (disease, decay, plague)
- Chemosh (death, the undead)
- Hiddukel (bargains, broken deals, greedy merchants)
- Nuitari (evil magic, black moon, patron of Black Robe wizards)
- Zeboim (the sea, storms, drowning)

### Krynn Geography
- **Ansalon**: Main continent (~1300 mi E-W, 900 mi N-S), primary setting
- **Taladas**: Second continent, less known to Ansalonians
- Before Cataclysm: solid landmass with great plains
- After Cataclysm: Blood Sea (where Istar was), New Sea, split Ergoth, changed coastlines
- Key regions: Solamnia (north), Abanasinia (central), Silvanesti (east), Qualinesti, Thorbardin (dwarven kingdom), Neraka (evil stronghold)

### Magic on Krynn
- Three Orders of High Sorcery: White Robes (good/Solinari), Red Robes (neutral/Lunitari), Black Robes (evil/Nuitari)
- Each order aligned to a moon — moon position affects spell power
- The Test of High Sorcery: rite of passage for wizards
- Tower of Wayreth: headquarters of the Conclave of Wizards

### Time Travel in DragonLance
**Legends Trilogy (Time of the Twins / War of the Twins / Test of the Twins)**:
- Raistlin Majere travels back in time to pre-Cataclysm Istar
- Assumes identity of ancient archmage Fistandantilus (killed him, absorbed his power)
- Creates a time loop — repeats Fistandantilus's historical actions
- Caramon and Tasslehoff follow him back via magical portal at Tower of Wayreth
- Kender/dwarves/gnomes "cannot go back in time" for fear of disrupting it (but Tas does anyway)
- The time-travel device (a magical artifact) is central to the plot
- Caramon and Tas are catapulted into a FUTURE where Raistlin destroyed the world
- They return to the present to stop Raistlin from leaving the Abyss
- Raistlin sacrifices himself to seal the portal and save Krynn

**Destinies Series (2022-2024)**:
- Destina Rosethorn uses magic to travel back in time
- Pulls iconic Dragonlance Companions physically back in time
- Characters find themselves with legendary figures from Krynn's past (Huma, Magius)

### DragonLance Design Lessons for World-Building
1. **Multiple Ages**: History divided into distinct eras separated by world-changing events
2. **The Cataclysm model**: A single pivotal event that reshapes geography, politics, and religion
3. **Gods as active agents**: Three families of gods who meddle directly in mortal affairs
4. **Balance as cosmic principle**: Good, Neutrality, and Evil must maintain equilibrium
5. **Time travel requires deep history**: When characters visit the past, the past must be fully realized
6. **Time loops**: Raistlin repeating Fistandantilus's actions — fate vs free will
7. **Paradox consequences**: Changing the past creates alternate futures (the devastated Krynn)
8. **Magical artifacts as plot devices**: The time-travel device, the dragonlance, the Portal to the Abyss
9. **Personal stakes within epic events**: The twins' relationship drives the Legends trilogy
10. **Kender as chaos agents**: Tasslehoff disrupts time because kender don't fear consequences

## 8. D&D Time Travel Mechanics

### Official 5e Time Travel Options
- **Wish** (9th level): Can potentially undo recent events, DM interprets consequences
- **Time Ravage** (9th level, Wildemount): Ages a creature to near-death, not actual travel
- **Chronurgy Magic** (Wildemount subclass): Convergent Future (force a reroll), Arcane Abeyance (store a spell)
- **Plane Shift / Gate**: Travel to planes with different temporal properties
- **Dream of the Blue Veil** (Tasha's): Experience visions of another Material Plane world
- **No official "teleport through time" spell** — DMs must homebrew this

### Homebrew Time Travel Best Practices
- Likely 7th level or higher spell
- Long casting time (minutes to hours)
- Rare/expensive material components
- Wisdom saving throw for accuracy of temporal jump
- Failed save = arrive at wrong time or location
- DM determines paradox consequences
- Limitations on how far back one can travel
- Potential for encountering alternate timeline versions of oneself
- Chronomancy magic items as alternative to spells
- Planar travel to planes with different temporal properties

### Key Time Travel Design Considerations for Campaigns
1. **Fixed timeline vs mutable past**: Can characters change history, or is it fixed?
2. **Paradox rules**: What happens if you meet yourself? Kill your grandfather?
3. **Butterfly effect**: Small changes cascade into large consequences
4. **Information asymmetry**: Characters from the present know what "will" happen
5. **Power level in the past**: Are characters more/less powerful in different eras?
6. **Return mechanics**: How do characters get back to their own time?
7. **Cultural differences**: The past is a different society with different norms
8. **Language barriers**: Do ancient languages differ from modern ones?

## 9. Homebrew and Custom Content

### DMs Guild vs Open Gaming License
- **DMs Guild**: Official marketplace for D&D content, uses WotC IP (Forgotten Realms, etc.), royalty-based
- **Creative Commons SRD 5.2**: Free to use core mechanics, classes, spells — no WotC IP
- Homebrew for personal use: DM can create anything for their own table

### Homebrew Design Principles (from DMG)
- Reskin existing options rather than creating new mechanics from scratch
- Use existing class features as models for new features
- Ask: How does this reinforce the theme? Is there an existing model? How does it compare in power?
- Best subclasses can be summed up in a single sentence
- Each feature should support the one-sentence theme
- Playtest before committing — "Slow is smooth, smooth is fast"

### Homebrew Categories
- **Custom races/species**: Use existing race mechanics as templates
- **Custom subclasses**: Follow class's subclass structure (e.g., Fighter gets features at 3/7/10/15/18)
- **Custom spells**: Compare to existing spells of same level for balance
- **Custom monsters**: Use CR system from DMG
- **Custom magic items**: Use DMG magic item tables as reference
- **Custom backgrounds**: Grant 2 skills, 1 tool, a feature, and equipment
- **House rules**: Modify existing rules (e.g., critical hits, death saves, resting)

## 11. World-Building Best Practices (from D&D Beyond, experts)

### 8 Tips from Lou Anders and Travis Vengroff
1. **Establish a goal** — what feeling do you want to evoke?
2. **Start small** — one village, one region, expand as needed
3. **Collaborate** — let players contribute to the world
4. **Take notes from history** — real historical events as inspiration (Game of Thrones = Wars of the Roses)
5. **Flesh out cultures** — not just geography, but how people live
6. **Think of places as characters** — each location has a personality
7. **Map out your world** — geography shapes trade, conflict, and culture
8. **Envision from player perspective** — what will they see and do?

### 10 Tips from Crit Academy
1. Diversity of locations — keep things fresh
2. Establish world flavor — bleak gothic or light whimsy?
3. Manage geography — topography should make sense
4. Don't show your full hand — let players discover the world
5. Provide wildlife — animals set the scene
6. Develop history — ancient and recent events make the world lived-in
7. Subvert expected tropes — surprise your players
8-10. (Player engagement, faction design, and thematic consistency)

### World-Building Step-by-Step (Runic Dice)
1. Choose a theme/concept
2. Outline geography (broad strokes first)
3. Create cultures (values, beliefs, customs)
4. Brief history and timeline (a few major events)
5. Design factions (conflicting interests)
6. Design the magic system (or use default D&D)
7. Populate 3-5 key locations (starting town, capital, dungeon, temple, wilderness)
8. Connect the world to player characters

## 12. D&D Pantheons Across Settings

### Forgotten Realms (Faerûn)
- 30+ widely known deities, many more local/cult gods
- Ao the Overgod above all
- Gods grouped by race (human pantheons, dwarven, elven, halfling, gnome, drow, orc)
- Four human proto-pantheons merged into the Faerûnian pantheon over millennia
- Key gods: Mystra (magic), Selûne (moon), Shar (darkness), Bane (tyranny), Tymora (luck), Tempus (war), Tyr (justice)

### Dragonlance (Krynn)
- Three families: 7 Good (Paladine), 7 Neutral (Gilean), 7 Evil (Takhisis)
- Gods don't have d20 mechanics directly — their "aspects" do
- Gods are formless, represent aspects of creation
- Three moons = three gods of magic (Solinari/white, Lunitari/red, Nuitari/black)
- Paladine = Bahamut on other worlds; Takhisis = Tiamat on other worlds

### Eberron
- Sovereign Host (unified pantheon of good/neutral gods) vs Dark Six (evil counterparts)
- Blood of Vol (divinity within mortals, reveres undead)
- Path of Light (glorious future belief)
- Undying Court (elven ancestor worship)
- Gods may or may not actually exist — faith matters, not verification

### Nonhuman Deities (shared across worlds)
- Moradin (dwarves), Corellon Larethian (elves), Yondalla (halflings), Garl Glittergold (gnomes), Gruumsh (orcs)
- These gods are revered on many different Material Plane worlds

## Summary for Campaign World Planning

This research provides the foundation for building a DragonLance-style campaign world. The key takeaways:

1. **Multiple Ages structure**: Like Krynn's 5 ages, divide history into eras separated by world-changing events
2. **Three-family pantheon**: Good/Neutral/Evil gods maintaining cosmic balance
3. **Cataclysm-type events**: Pivotal moments that reshape geography and society
4. **Time travel readiness**: Deep history so characters can visit past eras
5. **Active gods**: Deities who meddle in mortal affairs as agents of their alignment
6. **Magical artifacts**: Plot devices that enable time travel or world-changing events
7. **D&D 2024 rules**: Use the latest ruleset (Weapon Mastery, species not races, backgrounds for ability scores)
8. **Homebrew freedom**: DM can create custom races, classes, spells, monsters — the rules encourage it
9. **Multiverse connectivity**: Your world can exist in the D&D multiverse alongside Krynn, Toril, etc.
10. **Start small, expand**: Don't build the entire world at once — start with one region and grow
